A Nat Geo Project

We cannot fix what we cannot see.

Every monsoon, the ground beneath India’s most vulnerable communities becomes a minefield. Hidden hazards claim thousands of lives. The current system is blind to this. We are building a way to see it.

The Signal

The best sensors
don't use batteries.

Top-down municipal mapping fails because it relies on static surveys. But there is a demographic that walks these specific dirt paths, alleys, and kiln routes every single day. Children.

By treating migration, age, and language not as roadblocks, but as design variables, we transform marginalized youth into active, hyper-local data sensors.

The Architecture

Survival disguised as science.

If we teach a traditional safety class in environments that don't reward traditional learning, we fail. So, we are trying to design a "Need-Based" survival curriculum disguised as a mapping game.

Operational Model

Mirakle Couriers

Dhruv Lakra

Dhruv Lakra founded a logistics business entirely staffed by deaf adults. He recognized that deaf individuals possess heightened visual awareness and spatial memory, perfect traits for navigating complex urban routes.

Our Takeaway: Instead of treating deafness as a disability, he built an operational model around it as a core strength. This mirrors our approach to the children in the kilns. We aren't treating their age or lack of formal education as a barrier; we are using their innate local knowledge and daily physical presence as the ultimate environmental sensors.

Data Collection

Predictive Outbreaks

Umar Saif

During the 2012 Dengue epidemic in Lahore, Dr. Umar Saif realized high-tech tracking was failing. He gave basic smartphones to local street sweepers who simply clicked photos at the start and end of their shifts. The backend system used digital image processing to map clean vs. dirty areas, predicting hospital outbreaks and dropping cases from 16,000 to nearly zero the next year.

Our Takeaway: This is the exact blueprint for our project. We will give low-fi, accessible tools (like simple cameras and tracking cards) to ground-level participants (the children) to feed a high-level digital Vulnerability Index.

Pedagogical Theory

Barefoot College

Tilonia

Bunker Roy’s Barefoot College in Tilonia trains rural grandmothers from across the globe to become fully qualified solar engineers in six months. Almost all of these women have zero formal literacy. The program bypasses traditional text-based education entirely, using only visual cues, colors, and practical application.

Our Takeaway: This proves that we do not need a traditional classroom. We can successfully teach the children how to identify, track, and map critical environmental variables purely through visual literacy and context-based learning.
